### Abstract

The exploration of two-dimensional (2D) carbon allotropes as high-performance anode materials in Li-ion batteries (LIBs) has attracted experimental and theoretical research interest. Inspired by this, based on first-principles calculations, we comprehensively evaluate the viability of a novel 2D Orth-C14 monolayer regarded as a prospective anchoring material for LIBs.

The multifaceted analyses are conducted to unravel the stability and metallicity of the Orth-C14 monolayer. The calculations indicate that the Orth-C14 monolayer possesses the preferable anchoring strength ($-1.78\text{ eV}$) and charge transfer ($0.97\text{ e}$) toward lithium. To meet the requirement of an excellent anode material, the theoretical Li storage capacity of the Orth-C14 monolayer reaches up to $956\text{ mAh/g}$. More notably, the Orth-C14 monolayer exhibits an extremely low diffusion barrier ($0.12\text{ eV}$), and maintains suitable open circuit voltage ($0.053\text{--}0.393\text{ V}$). The Orth-C14 monolayer displays exceptional structural stability with only $1.3\%$ volume expansion during cycling, contributing to its outstanding cycle life.
