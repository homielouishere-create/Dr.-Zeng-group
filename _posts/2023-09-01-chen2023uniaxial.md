### Abstract

The electronic and optical properties of phenyl-functionalized black phosphorus (phenyl-BP) under uniaxial strain engineering were investigated using first-principles calculations.

It revealed that overall transitions from semiconductor to metallic properties were observed in phenyl-BP. Notably, when $4\%$ tensile strain was exerted along the armchair direction, phenyl-BP was restored to a p-type semiconductor with a smaller gap of $0.60\text{ eV}$. Furthermore, compressive strain enhanced its ability to absorb ultraviolet light and high photon energy region around $14\text{ eV}$. Our results suggested that the strain-induced properties of phenyl-BP provide theoretical guidance for realizing its full potential in optoelectronic applications.
