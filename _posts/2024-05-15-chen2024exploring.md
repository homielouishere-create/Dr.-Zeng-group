### Abstract

The two-dimensional material black phosphorene has attracted extensive attention due to its unique sensing performance. Herein, on the basis of first-principles calculations, we investigate the adsorption and dissociation of several toxic gases ($\text{NO}_2$, $\text{SO}_2$, $\text{H}_2\text{S}$) on the Li-decorated N-doped black phosphorene (LNBP).

Our results demonstrate that $\text{NO}_2$ and $\text{SO}_2$ have a strong interaction with LNBP due to the hybridization of O-2p with P-3p orbitals, while the $3\text{NO}_2$-LNBP system exhibits a high charge transfer up to $0.95\text{ e}$ from the Li atom to gas molecules. Furthermore, the capture ability of toxic gas on the LNBP follows the sequence of $\text{H}_2\text{S} < \text{SO}_2 < \text{NO}_2$, corresponding to adsorption energies of $-0.68\text{ eV}$, $-1.37\text{ eV}$, and $-2.55\text{ eV}$. In addition, LNBP is appropriate for $\text{NO}_2$ gas detection with high sensitivity ranging from $84.8\%$ to $92.6\%$ as coverage increases.
